cyber espionage Report 01

Fire Ant Hijacks Cisco Routers

China-linked cyber espionage group Fire Ant expanded its operations from VMware environments to Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS servers, and Linux management systems. Compromised routers were transformed into surveillance platforms for capturing network traffic, harvesting credentials, and concealing malicious activity from defenders. Attackers deployed specialized IOS XR implants, the TacTap credential-stealing toolkit, BridgeAgent backdoor, rootkits, and custom SSH backdoors. They also suppressed router logs, SNMP traps, authentication records, and Linux evidence to hinder investigations. Researchers observed probing of connected high-value environments, including critical infrastructure, although no confirmed compromise of those networks was identified during the investigation.

malware delivery Report 02

ValleyRAT Hides Inside Signed Adware

The Silver Fox threat actor is distributing ValleyRAT, also known as Winos 4.0, through a modified version of legitimate Chinese QN Wallpaper adware. The installer uses DLL sideloading, causing the signed QnWallpaper.exe process to load a malicious libcef.dll, helping malware evade signature-based trust controls. It also attempts to disable Windows Defender, establishes autorun persistence, and seeks administrator privileges. ValleyRAT provides extensive remote-control capabilities, including keystroke and clipboard collection, screenshots, and additional module delivery. Kaspersky recorded over 100,000 ValleyRAT-related detections during 2026, affecting more than 1,500 users, primarily across China and India.

ransomware activity Report 03

Aurora Ransomware Abuses Cursor AI

The Aurora ransomware group has incorporated the Cursor AI coding assistant into its intrusion workflow, demonstrating how legitimate generative AI tools can support cyberattacks. According to researchers, attackers used Cursor after gaining access to compromised environments to generate scripts and commands supporting discovery, credential access, lateral movement, security-control evasion, and ransomware deployment. The activity shows how AI-assisted development can reduce the expertise and time required to create customized tooling during an intrusion. Rather than exploiting a vulnerability in Cursor itself, the attackers abused legitimate capabilities. Defenders should monitor unusual AI-development-tool execution, scripting activity, credential access, and suspicious administrative behavior on enterprise endpoints.
