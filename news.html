ransomware activity Report 01

Hackers Target Qlik Sense Servers

Security researchers at Cynet identified coordinated attacks targeting Qlik Sense servers across multiple European organizations in October 2026. Attackers attempted to execute PowerShell reverse shells through Qlik's scheduler process, resembling techniques previously associated with CACTUS ransomware. Although initial attempts were blocked, researchers uncovered additional payloads enabling persistence through OpenSSH, scheduled tasks, and custom Go backdoors. Exposed attacker infrastructure contained exploits targeting Apache ActiveMQ (CVE-2023-46604) and Veeam (CVE-2024-40711), suggesting preparations for broader attacks. The initial exploitation method remains unidentified. Organizations should patch vulnerable services, monitor suspicious PowerShell activity, and investigate unauthorized remote connections.

certificate revocation Report 02

eMudhra Revokes Eight Additional Certificate Authorities

Indian certificate authority eMudhra revoked eight additional intermediate CAs following an investigation into its October 2026 cryptographic key compromise. Although their RSA private keys were not weakened, an internal Python script exposed sensitive key material in computer memory during a 2025 hardware security module migration. The company could not demonstrate compliance with its key protection requirements. Six affected authorities issued TLS certificates, while two supported code signing, collectively covering 195 certificates. eMudhra reports no confirmed misuse and has replaced affected certificates. Microsoft, Apple, Google, and Mozilla are withdrawing trust from affected certificate hierarchies, requiring organizations to review certificate dependencies.

data exposure Report 03

Thousands of Grok Apps Expose Secrets

Security researchers at Claroc identified 37,571 Grok-built applications, including 35,578 accessible without authentication. Approximately 10% contained internal business or personal information, while 2% exposed high-risk or critical data. Leaked information included employee records, patient details, confidential sales documents, and plaintext passwords. Twenty-four exposed applications belonged to employees of X and SpaceXSI, formerly xAI. Researchers also identified sensitive information associated with 30 other organizations, including six Fortune 500 companies. Grok's public-sharing configuration and discoverable application subdomains contributed to exposure risks. Organizations should audit AI-generated applications, enforce authentication, restrict sharing permissions, and remove exposed credentials.
