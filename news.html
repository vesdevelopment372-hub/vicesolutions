supply compromise Report 01

Polymarket Hack Steals $2.7 Million

Prediction market platform Polymarket suffered a supply chain attack on October 29, resulting in approximately $2.7 million stolen from 726 affected wallets. Attackers compromised DebugBear's DNS records, replacing a legitimate performance-monitoring script with malicious JavaScript. The injected code exploited authenticated trading sessions to transfer cryptocurrency positions or sell holdings before redirecting funds. Polymarket removed the script, suspended trading, reset active sessions, and initiated customer reimbursements totaling $3.9 million, including additional trading losses. DebugBear reported no confirmed compromise of its internal systems. Recommended defenses include Subresource Integrity, restrictive Content Security Policies, script sandboxing, and third-party dependency monitoring.

authentication bypass Report 02

Critical LXD Flaw Enables Client Impersonation

Canonical disclosed CVE-2026-112816, a critical vulnerability affecting LXD and MicroCloud, rated CVSS 10.0. The flaw allows attackers to impersonate trusted clients without possessing their private keys by manipulating TLS certificate chains. Exploitation requires network access to the HTTPS API and a trusted client's public certificate. Successful attacks could provide unauthorized cluster access, compromise virtual machines, and potentially grant root privileges on host systems. Canonical released patched LXD versions 6.10.1, 5.21.9, 5.0.11, and 4.0.15, alongside MicroCloud 3.4.1 and 2.1.5. No active exploitation has been reported. Administrators should update immediately and restrict API exposure.

cryptocurrency theft Report 03

North Korean Hackers Steal $6.43 Billion

The FBI attributed a record $6.43 billion cryptocurrency theft targeting Binance to North Korea-linked TraderTraitor, also known as Jade Sleet and UNC4899. Attackers stole assets across 11 blockchains, primarily Ethereum and Bitcoin. Approximately $2.85 billion has been frozen through blockchain enforcement measures. Investigators identified laundering activities involving Ethereum conversions, decentralized finance platforms, and instant swap services. TraderTraitor also uses fraudulent recruitment campaigns, AI-generated identities, and trojanized Terraform providers to distribute FLATROOF and ROOFDECK malware. The incident highlights sophisticated cryptocurrency theft operations and the importance of securing developer workflows, monitoring suspicious transactions, and strengthening supply chain defenses.
