Identity protection Report 01

Aadhaar Copies Increase Identity Theft Risk

Kolkata Police has advised travelers and hotel operators to avoid using unmasked Aadhaar cards as identity proof during hotel check-ins, warning that photocopies can be misused for identity theft, financial fraud, and unauthorized SIM registration if leaked. Instead, authorities recommend using the Masked Aadhaar or the official Aadhaar app for secure digital verification, limiting exposure of sensitive personal information. The advisory comes ahead of increased travel activity, when cybercriminals often exploit identity documents collected by hotels and travel agencies. Travelers should share only the minimum required information, verify hotel authenticity, and monitor accounts for suspicious activity.

AI vulnerability Report 02

AgentForger Creates Hidden AI Insiders

Security researchers disclosed AgentForger, a critical vulnerability in OpenAI ChatGPT Workspace Agents that enabled attackers to create and deploy hidden autonomous AI agents through a single phishing link. By exploiting weaknesses in the Agent Builder, attackers could silently provision an AI agent inheriting a victim employee's identity and permissions, granting access to enterprise resources such as email, cloud storage, and collaboration platforms. OpenAI addressed the vulnerability shortly after responsible disclosure. Organizations using Workspace Agents should ensure the latest fixes are applied, strengthen phishing defenses, monitor AI agent creation and permissions, and enforce governance policies for autonomous AI deployments.

Targeted phishing Report 03

BlueNoroff Profiles Victims Before Zoom Attacks

Security researchers uncovered a BlueNoroff campaign in which the North Korea-linked threat group profiles cryptocurrency and Web3 executives before launching highly convincing fake Zoom meetings. Attackers leverage stolen personal information, AI-generated avatars, and realistic meeting lures to build trust, then trick victims into executing malicious commands or installing malware. The operation enables credential theft, persistent access, and financial theft while reusing compromised victim identities to target additional organizations. The campaign demonstrates increasingly sophisticated social engineering combined with malware deployment. Organizations should verify meeting invitations, train employees against ClickFix-style attacks, restrict script execution, and monitor for suspicious remote administration activity.
