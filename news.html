supply compromise Report 01

Trezor Confirms Hidden Hardware Wallet Implants

Cryptocurrency hardware wallet manufacturer Trezor confirmed hidden implants in three Safe 5 devices purchased from former reseller CryptoBilis. The additional circuit boards intercept recovery phrases displayed during wallet setup or restoration and transmit encrypted copies through mobile networks to attacker-controlled servers. Trezor released firmware version 2.13.1 to prevent the identified implants from capturing new information, but previously exposed recovery phrases remain compromised. No associated financial losses have been reported. Customers who purchased devices from CryptoBilis since March should transfer funds to new wallets with new recovery phrases and contact Trezor Support for replacements.

hardware vulnerability Report 02

AMD Confidential VMs Face Secret Theft

Researchers from ETH Zurich and Google disclosed two attacks undermining AMD's SEV-SNP confidential computing technology. Mior (CVE-2026-0462) manipulates memory protection tables, allowing malicious hypervisors with BIOS control to read, modify, and forge virtual machine attestation reports. Skyfall (CVE-2026-40671) exploits processor side channels to recover sensitive register contents, including disk encryption keys. Both attacks were demonstrated against AMD EPYC processors under laboratory conditions. AMD has released firmware and microcode mitigations, with additional platform updates scheduled for November and December 2026. Cloud providers should deploy vendor BIOS updates and verify confidential computing protections. No active exploitation has been confirmed.

cryptocurrency theft Report 03

North Korean Deepfakes Steal $400 Million

The FBI revealed that North Korea-linked BlueNoroff hackers stole over $400 million in cryptocurrency through an 18-month social engineering campaign called FaceStealer. Attackers impersonated trusted contacts using prerecorded videos and AI-generated deepfakes during fraudulent Zoom and Microsoft Teams meetings. Fake technical errors prompted victims to install malware, exposing cryptocurrency wallets, credentials, and communication accounts. The campaign targeted 677 individuals across 58 countries, particularly cryptocurrency executives and developers. Authorities seized malicious infrastructure and over 900 victim webcam recordings. Organizations should independently verify meeting invitations, restrict untrusted software execution, and strengthen identity verification procedures.
