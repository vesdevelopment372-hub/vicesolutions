phishing-as-a-service Report 01

NovaCookies Steals Microsoft 365 Sessions

Researchers uncovered NovaCookies, a subscription-based adversary-in-the-middle phishing platform used against hundreds of organizations across multiple countries. Campaigns abuse genuine Docusign notifications containing malicious document-sharing lures, helping emails bypass sender reputation and authentication checks. Victims are redirected through legitimate Microsoft or Google authentication infrastructure before reaching attacker-controlled phishing pages. NovaCookies proxies Microsoft 365 authentication in real time, capturing passwords, MFA codes, and authenticated session information. The service costs about $320 per month and includes anti-analysis capabilities designed to evade security scanners. Researchers assess NovaCookies as a variant of the Sneaky 2FA phishing kit.

nation-state cyber espionage Report 02

FBI Disrupts China-Linked QTFY Network

The FBI and U.S. Department of Justice disrupted QScan and QTRouter, hacking platforms operated by the China-linked QTFY group. QScan scans for vulnerable networks and compromises IoT devices, while QTRouter combines infected devices, commercial proxies, and VPS infrastructure to conceal the real origin of cyberattacks. Victims reportedly included NASA, the Federal Reserve, Department of Energy, Department of Justice, NIH, and U.S. Senate. The group exploited both zero-day and known vulnerabilities to gain access before deploying RATs and web shells. Court-authorized domain seizures disrupted the platforms by taking control of domains hard-coded into their infrastructure.

cyber espionage Report 03

Nimbus Manticore Expands Espionage Arsenal

Researchers uncovered new malware and infrastructure linked to Nimbus Manticore, an Iranian state-sponsored group associated with the IRGC. The expanded arsenal includes a reverse SSH tunneling utility disguised as a Windows Terminal Server component and a C++ backdoor resembling the previously documented TWOSTROKE malware. The backdoor communicates with hard-coded C2 servers over HTTPS and supports system reconnaissance, file upload and download, DLL and executable execution, directory enumeration, and file deletion. Researchers identified infrastructure spanning the Middle East and Europe, indicating an expanding targeting profile and continued investment in tools designed to maintain persistent access to compromised organizations.
