AI Threats Report 01

AI Agents Accelerate Automated Cyberattacks

Google Threat Intelligence Group reports that cybercriminals are increasingly deploying autonomous AI agents to automate vulnerability exploitation and multistage cyberattacks. Frameworks including ARTEX, CAIRN, CyberStrikeAI, and PentAGI enable attackers to accelerate reconnaissance, exploit development, and intrusion activities. Threat actors also use AI-powered differential analysis to reverse-engineer software patches and rapidly weaponize disclosed vulnerabilities. Additionally, attackers exploit compromised cloud resources through LLMjacking and leverage AI-assisted phishing platforms to customize malicious campaigns. These developments significantly reduce exploitation timelines, increase attack scalability, and challenge traditional defensive response capabilities. Organizations must prioritize rapid patching, continuous monitoring, AI infrastructure protection, and proactive threat detection.

OT Vulnerabilities Report 02

Unpatched Danfoss Flaws Threaten Refrigeration Systems

Security researchers at Midnight Blue discovered six unpatched vulnerabilities affecting Danfoss AK-SM800A refrigeration controllers, potentially enabling unauthenticated remote takeover and root-level code execution. Five flaws affect unauthenticated web handlers, while another compromises Strict mode protections. Firmware version 5.4.0 reportedly remains vulnerable, with no official patches or CVE identifiers available. Successful exploitation could manipulate refrigeration temperatures, trigger defrost cycles, conceal malicious activity, and cause significant financial losses. Although earlier refrigeration attacks disrupted European facilities, exploitation of these specific flaws remains unconfirmed. Security agencies recommend immediately removing internet exposure, implementing VPN-based access, monitoring configuration changes, and strengthening network segmentation.

Malware Threats Report 03

Retadup Worm Resurfaces After Seven Years

Kaspersky researchers identified the resurgence of Retadup, a Windows worm disrupted by French authorities in 2019, now distributing CoffeeLoader malware. Attackers re-registered former command-and-control domains to reactivate malware distribution, initially spreading SmokeLoader before switching to CoffeeLoader in 2025. The updated loader uses GPU-assisted decryption, malicious DLL files, registry persistence, and process injection to evade detection. Retadup primarily propagates through infected USB drives, exposing Windows systems to additional malicious payloads. Kaspersky reportedly blocked approximately 15,000 infections, particularly across Peru, Angola, Russia, and Bolivia. Organizations should strengthen removable-media controls, endpoint monitoring, and malware detection capabilities.
