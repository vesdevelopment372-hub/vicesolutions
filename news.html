Apple Tightens macOS Full Disk Access

Apple plans to strengthen Full Disk Access (FDA) controls in future macOS releases by requiring applications to meet additional verification requirements before receiving broad access to protected user data. FDA can expose sensitive information from Mail, Messages, Safari, backups, and other protected locations, making it highly valuable to information-stealing malware. The proposed changes aim to prevent untrusted or malicious software from abusing social engineering to obtain these privileges while preserving legitimate access for security and management applications. The move continues Apple’s broader effort to harden macOS privacy controls and reduce the impact of increasingly sophisticated infostealers targeting browser data, credentials, cryptocurrency wallets, and other sensitive information.

credential security Report 02

Credential Sprawl Outpaces Security Visibility

Modern enterprises face an expanding credential attack surface as developers, applications, infrastructure, automation, and AI agents generate and consume increasing numbers of secrets. GitGuardian detected 28.65 million new hardcoded secrets in public GitHub commits during 2025, while AI-service credential leaks increased 81%. Internal repositories were about six times more likely than public repositories to contain secrets, and 28% of secret incidents originated outside source-code repositories. Developer endpoints add further risk as infostealers and AI agents can access local credentials. Organizations need continuous credential discovery across repositories, endpoints, and collaboration systems, combined with ownership mapping, permission analysis, rotation, and prevention controls.

IoT botnet Report 03

Realtek Flaw Deploys Cling Botnet

Researchers observed a surge in exploitation of CVE-2021-35394, a critical CVSS 9.8 remote code execution flaw affecting the Realtek Jungle SDK, beginning around September 5, 2026. Some attacks deploy a botnet called Cling, which contains exploits for multiple router and DVR vulnerabilities and can propagate in a worm-like manner. Cling establishes persistence, provides TCP tunneling and proxy capabilities, and can launch denial-of-service attacks. Its distinctive feature is abuse of STUN, a legitimate NAT-traversal protocol, for command-and-control communications, allowing malicious network activity to resemble ordinary STUN traffic and making conventional monitoring more difficult.
